# RSHallu: Dual-Mode Hallucination Evaluation for Remote-Sensing Multimodal Large Language Models with Domain-Tailored Mitigation

## Problem
Multimodal LLMs used on remote sensing imagery hallucinate, and existing hallucination benchmarks (such as POPE [A029], RSPOPE) are object-centric. Remote sensing adds failures at the level of the whole image: misjudging sensor modality, resolution, or the overall land-use scene.

## Approach
- Taxonomy: object-level hallucination (existence, attributes, relations) plus a new image-level class split into image attributes (modality, resolution) and image scene (land use, global semantics).
- RSHalluEval: 2,023 QA pairs built on RSIEval (expert QA kept and re-categorised) plus 186, 496, 100 and 298 new pairs written by three experts on RSIEval, RSITMD, UCM-Captions and Sydney-Captions images. Images are colour and panchromatic optical only. Many added questions are misleading (nonexistent objects, attributes or interactions) or have negative answers.
- Scoring: hallucination-free (HF) rate per category and overall. Experts score each answer 0, 0.5 (cannot decide, e.g. very low resolution) or 1. Expert scoring uses 300 randomly sampled QA pairs per model; automatic checkers score all pairs with a binary judgement.
- Dual-mode checking: online, Qwen-VL-Max with chain-of-thought and the ground-truth answer; local, a Qwen2-VL-7B checker LoRA-tuned (rank 8) on RSHalluCheck (15,396 QA pairs from 1,261 RSICap images; 13,994 train, 1,402 validation; generated by Qwen-VL-Max, 92% correct in a 100-pair audit). The local checker does not see the ground truth.
- Mitigation: RSHalluShield, 30,000 QA pairs (15,000 normal, 15,000 misleading) from 1,292 other RSICap images (86% correct in a 100-pair audit) for LoRA fine-tuning; training-free decoding-time logit correction that mixes in selected late intermediate layers (29 to 31); and two prompts, an "overall perception" one-sentence description step and a "counterfactual perception" warning that the question may contain false premises.

## Data and benchmarks
Evaluated models: general LLaVA-1.5, mPLUG-Owl3, Qwen2-VL; RS-specific GeoChat, LHRS-Bot, LHRS-Bot-Nova, VHM (7B where local). Downstream transfer on RSVQA-LR/HR and DIOR-RSVG.

## Key results
- Expert HF_all (Table 1): mPLUG-Owl3 0.6900, VHM 0.6833, Qwen2-VL 0.6783, LHRS-Bot-Nova 0.6700, LLaVA-1.5 0.5317, GeoChat 0.4417, LHRS-Bot 0.3633. RS-specific range is therefore 0.3633 to 0.6833; the top overall score belongs to a general model.
- Image-attribute HF (expert, Table 1) is very uneven: VHM 0.9130 and LHRS-Bot-Nova 0.7273, but mPLUG-Owl3 and LHRS-Bot only 0.1667 and LLaVA-1.5 0.3750.
- Checkers: chain-of-thought with an "accuracy" marking rule gives the lowest mean relative error against experts, ES_all 0.0637 (Table 2). The fine-tuned local Qwen2-VL checker reaches 0.8716 accuracy on RSHalluCheck validation (Table 3) but ES_all 0.2844 against experts on RSHalluEval, with ES on image attributes of 1.24 (Table 4). It ran in 266 s versus 3,601 s for the online mode without CoT or ground truth.
- Decoding correction: small gains, largest for GeoChat (HF_all 0.4048 to 0.4305 greedy, Table 5).
- Prompting (Table 6, scored by the online CoT checker): counterfactual prompt raises LLaVA-1.5 HF_all from 0.5007 to 0.5699; combining both prompts gives 0.6090. The headline "21.63" is this relative gain (0.6090 / 0.5007), equal to about 10.8 percentage points absolute; the abstract calls it percentage points, which does not match Table 6.
- Fine-tuning Qwen2-VL on RSHalluShield raises HF_all from 0.7044 to 0.7731 (Table 7) but lowers image-scene HF slightly, because generated answers are sentences while expert answers are single words.
- Downstream: Qwen2-VL-HF gets RSVQA-LR average 92.71 (Table 9), RSVQA-HR zero-shot average 71.58 (Table 10) and DIOR-RSVG precision at IoU 0.5 of 78.04 versus 76.78 for MGVLF (Table 11).

## Limitations
- Unpublished preprint; code and data not released at reading time.
- Expert HF rates rest on 300 sampled answers per model, and automatic scores rely on an MLLM judge whose error is itself measured against those experts.
- Optical colour and panchromatic imagery only; no SAR, multispectral indices or multi-temporal change questions.
- Mitigation data are machine-generated with roughly 86% audited correctness.

## What it means for EvidEO
- Cite the expert table as evidence that even the best models hallucinate on roughly a third of answers, with RS-specific models no better than general ones.
- The weak image-attribute scores are directly relevant: an explanation layer must not be trusted to state sensor, polarisation or resolution. EvidEO should copy these from the receipt and check them automatically.
- Copy the dual-mode idea cheaply: a deterministic local checker that compares every number and date in the VLM's explanation against the evidence card fields.
- Their counterfactual prompt is a cheap, testable add-on for EvidEO's parser when users ask about things that are not in the data.
- Report a hallucination-free rate for EvidEO explanations on an Indian question set.

## Findings log (full text read in Chrome; newest last)

Method: each paper's HTML full text (and tables) loaded with Claude in Chrome from arxiv.org/html, then compared with the archive entry, the draft (`paper/evideo.tex`) and `NOVELTY.md`. Supplementary material past the 50,000-character page-text limit was NOT loaded unless stated. Figures were not viewed unless stated.

### Batch 1 (draft-cited entries that were checked from abstracts or API only)

**A202 SHRUG-FM (2511.10370): holds, one nuance.**
- Confirmed: flood AURC 0.115 +/- 0.001 on WorldFloods test (Table 2); burn Risk@0.5 0.090 vs 0.137 (MI); "we do not additionally calibrate the uncertainties"; split sizes 39,060/1,854/1,936 (flood), 225/41/68 (landslide); depth-3 tree; k=64; failure = F1 below 0.6 (0.5 landslides).
- Nuance not in our text: on floods SHRUG-FM is NOT best at 75% coverage (Risk@0.75 0.149 vs MI 0.139, AE 0.142) and ties at 50% (0.107 vs 0.108). Its own ablation (Table 3) shows dropping the input signals gives a better flood AURC (0.104 vs 0.114, the "input paradox"). So "lowest AURC" is right, "consistently outperforms" (the paper's wording) is generous.
- Venue "CVPR 2026 EarthVision" is not in the page text; it comes from the arXiv comment and was not re-checked.

**A205 OpenEarthAgent (2602.17665): numbers hold; NOVELTY.md has an error.**
- Confirmed: Table 3 step-by-step 99.51/97.18/96.08/62.10 (Summ 83.64; o4-mini Summ 89.48); Table 4 end-to-end answer accuracy 45.26 vs 13.72 (Qwen3-4B), 39.22 (GPT-4o), 43.88 (GPT-5); tool-order about 67-72%; 60-query subset 60.04 vs 21.75, 21.67 s vs 933.54 s; ThinkGeo table 16.35 vs 9.78 reported; 14,538 train / 1,169 eval; 100,656 steps.
- **Error:** `NOVELTY.md` (row for A205 and the run-9 paragraph) says it has "no SAR". The paper trains on SAR datasets (SSDD, SADD, SIVED: ship, aircraft, vehicle detection) and says it covers "optical and SAR operations". Correct wording: SAR only as object-detection chips, no SAR water or flood analysis, no sensor switching.
- Not confirmed in the loaded text (supplement not loaded): "about 45 GB runtime memory"; "ECCV 2026" venue.
- Draft sentence ("replays tool traces ... leave the final answer's reliability unstated") is supported: deterministic replay is in the abstract and 3.2; no confidence or abstention anywhere in the text.

**A201 TerraScope (2603.19039): numbers hold; NOVELTY.md has an error.**
- Confirmed: Table 1 TerraScope-Bench avg 68.9 vs 59.6 / 54.9 / 43.3 / GPT-4o 38.7; DisasterM3 avg 46.5 vs 38.8; Table 2 ablation 58.7 / 43.2 / 62.8 / 68.9; IoU 0.628 vs 0.443, r = 0.607; limitations (hallucination, RGB and SAR only, bi-temporal); no confidence or abstention in the main text.
- **Error:** `NOVELTY.md` (row for A201) says "Reads optical or SAR, no automatic choice". The method does choose: text-guided token-level modality selection, optical where clear and SAR where cloud-covered (Section 3.2, Fig. 7 cloud example). Caveats that keep the novelty claim alive: it is learned per token by text relevance, not a cloud test; concatenation fusion scored slightly higher (Table 3); applies to prepared chips, not live scene search.
- Not confirmed in the loaded text (appendix not loaded): "2.48 s per sample, 22.4 GB peak on an A100" (main text says H200 GPUs were used for experiments); "optical plus SAR subset 41.5 vs 19.6"; the "inconsistent hyperparameters" note; "CVPR 2026" venue.

**A204 UnivEARTH (2504.12110): numbers hold, with caveats.**
- Confirmed (Tables 3 and 4, Sections 4.1-4.4): Claude-4.5-Sonnet 40.0%, Gemini-2.5-Pro 36.9%, GPT-5 33.1%; Flash 10.6, Haiku 16.7, Kimi 17.0; syntax errors 21.4% for the best model and over 30% for others; empty-collection errors about 15%; VIIRS accuracy 38.2-61.5% with documentation; Reflexion lifts Claude 40.0 to 64.5 and GPT-5 33.1 to 59.3, but wrong answers also rise 15.7 to 26.0%.
- The paper says "79 percentage point increase" for GPT-5; the table gives about 26 points (79% relative). Do not copy its wording.
- Limits that matter to us: yes/no questions only, no unanswerable questions, CS-student reviewers. Table 1 lists only 2 Sentinel-2 questions and no Sentinel-1; the table total (443) differs from the 408 questions in the text.
- "ACL 2026 Findings" venue is not in the page text. Draft sentence "agents fetch live data" is supported (they write Earth Engine code).

**A176 EO-Gym (2605.01250): numbers hold; NOVELTY.md and the draft overstate the SAR switching.**
- Confirmed (Tables 2, 3, 5, 7, 9): 9,078 trajectories, 34,604 steps, 35 tools, over 660k files, test 1,436 / train 7,642; EO-Gym-4B Pass@1/2/3 0.65/0.71/0.74 vs base 0.42/0.47/0.49; Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.67 at Pass@3 (CI [0.65, 0.69] below [0.71, 0.76]); Pass@10 0.80; Unverified/All-tools 0.69; Detailed prompt: GPT-5.1 slightly ahead at Pass@2/3 (0.66/0.70 vs 0.66/0.69).
- **Archive entry is right, NOVELTY.md is generous.** Task families are Temporal, Disaster Impact (xBD buildings), Geospatial, Visual Understanding, Spatial Navigation, Object Counting. There is no flood-extent task. Sentinel-1 is cited as a sensor reference only; the SAR files come from M4-SAR and SARDet-100K, and "imagery" is Landsat and Sentinel-2.
- "Cross-modal switching" is a tool the agent calls to align an optical view with a SAR view from a local pre-indexed lake ("all retrieval operations are implemented as local lookups"). Nothing in the text says it chooses SAR because of cloud; the introduction only motivates it that way. In the transfer test (Table 8) cross-modal Pass@1 gets slightly worse after fine-tuning (0.731 to 0.712).
- Correct wording for NOVELTY.md row and `paper/evideo.tex` line 61: "EO-Gym trains a model to call optical-to-SAR retrieval tools inside a local benchmark" instead of "learns to switch to SAR". The N6 "not unique alone" verdict can stay but the "learned switch" label should go.
- No confidence, abstention or calibration anywhere in the text. Supports the "leaves reliability unstated" sentence.
- Oddity: the author list on the page has emails that do not match the names (for example Sai Ma with sai.ma@anu.edu.au is fine, but the next four emails belong to other authors in the list). Likely a template slip; the dataset account is "paperuploadacount". Not a reason to drop the paper, but worth a mention if cited as a main comparator.
- Not loaded: appendix (page text capped at 50,000 of 128,321 characters).

**A227 Kolluru et al. (2606.07780): holds; read from the arXiv PDF (no HTML version), main text only.**
- Confirmed: 19 events, GFM mean F1 0.61 and IoU 0.46 (DSWx: n=12, F1 0.65, IoU 0.49); cropland IoU 52% / F1 69%; tree cover and built-up IoU 4% / F1 9%; riverine F1 0.69 (n=6), cyclone 0.56 (n=7), flash flood 0.55 (n=3); 23 failure modes in four pipeline stages; F1 above 0.7 for 10 of 19 events.
- Archive wording "about 0.55" for cyclone and flash is fine (0.56 and 0.55). Sample sizes are small (3 flash events), which the archive entry should say.
- Useful extra not in the archive entry: a fragmentation index below 5.0 went with F1 above 0.7 (mean 4.4 for good events vs 15.4 for F1 below 0.5), and the authors propose it as a flag for manual review. That is a cheap, label-free reliability signal we could test.
- The paper says the weak regimes (vegetated, urban, cyclone under cloud) reflect both optical limits and training bias toward riverine open terrain, and that SAR is the recommended fallback. This supports the SAR-first rule but is not evidence about SAR error rates.
- Not loaded: supplementary figures S3 to S5.

**A033 RSHallu (2602.10799): table numbers hold; the "percentage points" wording is wrong.**
- Confirmed (Table 1, expert rows, overall hallucination-free rate HFall): LHRS-Bot 36.33, GeoChat 44.17, LHRS-Bot-Nova 67.00, Qwen2-VL 67.83, VHM 68.33, mPLUG-Owl3 69.00. 2,023 QA pairs, 15,396 check pairs, 30,000 shield pairs (15,000 normal and 15,000 misleading). The checker is Qwen-VL-Max with chain-of-thought (online) and a fine-tuned Qwen2-VL (local).
- **Error in the abstract and in our archive entry:** "up to 21.63 percentage points" is a relative gain. Table 6 shows LLaVA-1.5 going from 0.5007 to 0.6090 HFall with both prompting strategies, which is +10.8 points, or +21.6% relative (0.6090/0.5007 = 1.2163). The text then says "an observed increase of 21.63%". Our archive entry copied the abstract; it is now corrected.
- Image-attribute hallucination (modality, resolution) is the weak spot: expert HFIA is 0.17 for LHRS-Bot and mPLUG-Owl3 even where the overall rate is 0.69. This supports checking sensor and resolution words against the receipt.
- The automatic judges disagree with the experts by up to several points (for example LLaVA-1.5 0.5317 expert-column vs 0.5007 API-CoT vs 0.7084 local checker, rows are interleaved in the PDF text and need the PDF figure to confirm which is which). Treat any single-judge number as soft.
- Code and data "will be released"; still unreleased at archive time. Supplement not read. Figures not viewed.

**A014 GEOBench-VLM (2411.19325): holds; read from the arXiv PDF text, main text and appendix dataset list.**

- The draft's "best referring-expression score is 34%" is right: Table 4, Sphinx Prec@0.5 = 0.3408 (Prec@0.25 = 0.5289). The Fig. 1b bar for GPT-4o is also right: Prec@0.5 = 0.0087, i.e. under 1%. EarthDial 0.2429, GeoChat 0.1151, Qwen2-VL 0.1518.
- Resolves the archive's open question on SAR: the benchmark does include non-optical tasks (SAR sets So2Sat and QuakeSet; earthquake magnitude estimation, Table 5) and multispectral and bi-temporal and multi-temporal inputs. Still no flood-extent or Indian-geography focus.

- Not checked: per-task figures in Fig. 4; supplementary prompts.

**A213 Krullikowski et al., JSTARS 2023 (IEEE Xplore 10186373): holds, and the full text is open access (CC BY); read in Chrome, first 50,000 of 60,548 characters (Sections I-VI up to the Q-Q discussion). Conclusion and the rest of the discussion not loaded. Figures not viewed.**
- Confirmed, previously unread: mean of three harmonised likelihoods, unweighted, nodata ignored; 0-100 range, 50 threshold; DLR fuzzy value uses backscatter, slope and minimum mapping unit, defuzzification at 60, rescaled by likelihood = 100 - 1.25(100 - F) for F >= 60 and F/1.2 below; TUW conditional error inverted (100 - U for flood pixels, U for non-flood), with an internal mask at conditional error 0.2 (a 4:1 probability); LIST uses the minimum of water and change probabilities and a same-orbit reference scene. Majority vote with "split" cases resolved to non-flood. Excluded pixels set to 0, blob-removed and excluded flooded pixels set to 49. Exclusion layer = radar shadow, no SAR sensitivity, too far from drainage.
- Evaluation is thinner than the abstract suggests: one quantile-quantile plot (Fig. 5) for Myanmar against a manually mapped Sentinel-2 flood extent one day later, plus a qualitative Somalia non-flood case. No accuracy, F1 or calibration error is reported. The text itself says most samples have higher predicted than empirical probability (overdetection) and that the empirical probability peaks at only 0.9.
- So the GFM likelihood is a confidence index checked by one plot, not a calibrated probability. The archive limitation ("not shown to mean 70%") is right and is now stated in the paper's own terms. The draft sentence "combines three SAR algorithms into an ensemble likelihood" is accurate and does not claim calibration.
- Archive wording to correct: "neither in South Asia" is true (Myanmar is South-East Asia); "numeric scores were not read" can now read "the paper reports none beyond the Q-Q plot".
- The DLR algorithm uses slope from Copernicus DEM, and GFM excludes radar shadow and areas far from drainage: supports adding a terrain mask in EvidEO (already in SOLUTION.md risk 12 and 15).

### Batch 2 (15 papers: 13 read in full text, 1 abstract only, 1 not obtainable)

Method: PDFs fetched with curl into a temp folder and converted with pdftotext (arXiv, CVF, a Purdue copy of Otsu, a CiteSeerX copy of Platt's 1999 preprint). **A022 Sen1Floods11: numbers hold.**
- Table 1 and 2 values match our archive and the draft table (Otsu-VH 0.359 test and 0.386 Bolivia, all-water column). Hand labels come from Sentinel-2 maps corrected by analysts, so the reference is optical-derived. The paper's own Otsu baseline uses 1 km blocks chosen by variance and a focal-mean smoothing of VH, which our instrument lacks.
- Paper errors: 4,370 vs 4,385 weak chips (4,831 - 446 = 4,385); Otsu cited as Automatica 1975.

**A041 Kuro Siwo: numbers hold; licence in our entry was wrong.**
- Table 2 values match. Licence is MIT per the paper (our entry said CC BY). Asia has fewer events but more samples than Europe, so "underrepresented" was too strong.
- The GRD product is speckle-filtered and terrain-corrected through SNAP; our change calibration is fitted on that, which a live undespeckled scene is not. CEMS vs Kuro Siwo IoU 51%/48% on one French event.

**A001 GeoChat: numbers hold; our draft sentence needs a caveat (applied).**
- The 90.7% on RSVQA-LR leaves out count and area questions, so it says nothing about numeric answers. The printed average (90.70) is also not the mean of its columns (91.81, the figure LHRS-Bot's paper uses). The 10.6% grounding benchmark is built by the authors from templated expressions.
- FloodNet is in the training mix and Fig. 5 shows a confident flood answer; no flood score is reported.

**A003 EarthDial: numbers hold; SAR evidence is thin.**
- 69.94 BigEarthNet is the multispectral figure (RGB 68.82). SAR tests are ship detection and an earthquake yes/no (57.53 vs 55.86 for GPT-4o). No SAR flood or water task. No confidence or abstention.

**A002 EarthGPT: numbers hold; one claim in our entry not found.**
- 77.37 and 74.72 hold. SAR data are three ship-detection sets; SAR evidence is qualitative. Our entry's "acknowledges pixel-level localization difficulty" is not in the text; replaced with the stated future work.

**A005 LHRS-Bot: numbers hold; text overstates against GPT-4.**
- 89.19, 92.55, 80.78, 39.38 hold (the last checked on the page image). In Table 5 GPT-4-Turbo scores 44.56, so "matches GPT-4V" is generous. Quantity 18.25 and Area 25.33 are its weakest dimensions. VQA tables again omit count and area. A qualitative figure says it can express uncertainty; no calibration.

**A011 RSVQA: numbers hold; our entry mislabelled AA as overall.**
- LR AA 79.08 and OA 81.49; HR set 2 AA 78.23, OA 76.33. With a random image the model still scores 72.51 on the held-out city (OA 76.33 with the right image): answers can be nearly image-independent.

**A012 VRSBench: numbers hold.**
- Grounding 57.4 unique, 49.8 all; VQA 77.8; quantity about 58.8 at best. Annotations were drafted by GPT-4V then human-checked (1,004 hours); scorers use GPT-4. GPT-4V and untuned GeoChat were not fine-tuned, unlike the README summary.

**A026 Guo et al. (calibration): holds; two small draft points.**
- ECE uses 15 bins; the draft uses 10 (fine, but do not imply Guo used 10). All results are in distribution; the paper never tests calibration under dataset shift, which is the gap our leave-one-event-out result addresses.

**A027 Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017: draft citation was wrong for AURC (fixed).**
- The paper defines risk, coverage, the risk-coverage curve and the SGR guarantee, not AURC. AURC is defined in Geifman, Uziel and El-Yaniv, ICLR 2019 (arXiv 1805.08206; read the relevant passages). Draft now cites both; `refs.bib` has the 2019 entry.

**A028 RAPS: draft citation was wrong for conformal risk control (fixed).**
- RAPS gives marginal coverage of label sets under exchangeability; it is not conformal risk control. The draft's "next steps" sentence now cites Conformal Risk Control (entry 133; `refs.bib` entry added). Exchangeability is what a new region may break.

**A126 Platt (1999 preprint of the same chapter): holds.**
- Pages 1 to 5 viewed as images. Sigmoid 1/(1+exp(Af+B)) fitted by cross-entropy; fitting on the training data is biased, use hold-out or cross-validation. Pages 6 to 11 not read.

**A108 Otsu 1979: holds.**
- Full text read from a Purdue copy. It also defines a separability measure eta* (0 to 1, scale-invariant) as a bimodality measure, a possible cheaper alternative to Ashman's D. No radar content.

**A083 Twele 2016: abstract only.** Paywalled; DLR eLib has no full text; Semantic Scholar lists no open copy. Abstract: two test sites of one Greece-Turkey flood, overall accuracy 94.0 to 96.1%, kappa 0.879 to 0.910, VV slightly better than VH in calm wind. Our "follows the split-based approach" rests on the abstract and the GFM description.

**A065 Lee and See 2004: not read.** Paywalled; no open copy found (Semantic Scholar: closed). The draft cites it for "people rely on automation in proportion to how confident it appears". That framing is the common reading of the paper but is unconfirmed here; a copy from the user's library would settle it.
